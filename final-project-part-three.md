# The final data story

The final data story I created is a Shorthand storyboard which can be accessed via https://chongdiw-dataviz.shorthandstories.com/fa1b5f6b-df97-496a-81a4-ffdc044a3911/.

# Changes made since Part II

Following the interviews and reflection process in Part II, I have made several improvements to my final design:

- Several Pittsburgh-specific sections are added, including the preface which states the abundance of Chinese restaurants in the CMU area, the distribution of Chinese restaurants in Pittsburgh, as well as my personal recommendation list of eateries;
- All data sources are reexamined, with more solid immigration and restaurant data from the Western PA Data Center, South Connecticut State University and the US Census Bureau being included into data charts;
- Exploring more diversified data charts like a live map enabled by Tableau's strong geospatial functionalities.

On the other hand, compromises had to be made elsewhere. Due to limitations in project time and software capabilities, I had to scrap the idea of using a pictorial unit chart or a dot chart to depict the growth of restaurants over time, and chose a simple stacked bar chart instead. After all, I believe that what matters most is not the variety of chart types, but rather the effectiveness of the ideas conveyed.

## The audience

I decided that my audience would be restaurant-goers and food lovers in Pittsburgh's CMU-Oakland area, which is reflected in my final presentation and storyboard contents and in accordance with my Part II thought process.

# Final thoughts

Chinese cuisine and restaurants in the US is an inherently broad topic that deserves a dedicated course or a book to present, since it is heavily intertwined with the history of the lives, dreams, struggles and endeavors of Chinese immigrants in an alien land on the opposite side of the earth, as well as the complex, ever-changing relationship between two of the world's largest nations. To combine such a story-rich topic with the context of data visualization and Pittsburgh-specific contents requires even more historical knowledge, cultural mastery and data-empowered mindset. Therefore, I would definitely love to explore ways of making my data visualization more effective and to dive deeper into the backstage stories of how Chinese cuisine and restaurants have settled, grown and matured in Pittsburgh, even after this course ends. That said, given the tight timeline of this project, and that this is my first time using advanced dataviz tools and techniques to present a story, I'm already satisfied with my final deliverables.
